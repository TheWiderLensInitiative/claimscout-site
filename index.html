Why we built it

Money slips through the cracks

Settlements don't always pay you directly. Many send a virtual prepaid card or a "choose your payment" link by email, and those expire. They often land in Spam. Subscription apps help you file, then leave you to notice the payout yourself.

ClaimScout was built after exactly that: a settlement paid out as a virtual card, the email sat unnoticed, and by the time it was opened the card was worth $0. So ClaimScout doesn't stop at filing. It keeps watching until you're paid.

💳
Virtual card found in Spam

For example, a $38.50 card with 38 days left to redeem. You get a high-priority alert, not a surprise later.

👆
"Choose your payment" link

PayPal, Venmo or card. ClaimScout tells you before the link expires.

📝
"More information needed"

Deficiency notices are flagged with their reply-by date, so a claim isn't denied for a missing answer.

🚫
Ads and newsletters ignored

"Claim your share!" promos, debt collectors and wallet emails don't set off false alarms.

Features

Everything the paid apps do, and the part they forget

From finding settlements to getting the money, on hardware you already own.

🧭

A quick questionnaire

ClaimScout turns the open settlements into simple "have you used this?" questions, with company logos. Your answers build your profile once, and every new settlement is matched to it automatically.

🔎

Hundreds of settlements

Reads the public lists from Top Class Actions, ClassAction.org and OpenClassActions every 6 hours and merges duplicates, so each settlement appears once with its payout, deadline and official claim link.

✅

Proof needed, or not

Every card says whether you need proof of purchase. Many settlements need none: file with just the form.

📧

Finds proof in your email

Need a receipt? The phone searches your Gmail or Outlook, read-only, for emails from that company, and pulls out the order number, date and amount.

The important one

💸 Payout watcher

Every 4 hours it checks your inboxes, Spam included, for virtual cards, payment links, checks and requests for more information, then alerts you before they expire.

🗂️

Claim tracker

Mark a claim as filed with its claim number and notes. It moves to My claims, and you can update it to approved, paid or denied.

🌍

On home Wi-Fi it just works. Add free Tailscale and the app reaches your PC from anywhere, no port forwarding.

🤖

Optional AI, free

Off by default. Turn it on with a model on your own PC (Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp) or free OpenRouter models for smarter matching and proof reading.

How it works

Your PC does the work. Your phone keeps you posted.

Collect

Your PC reads the public settlement lists every 6 hours: eligibility, proof, payout, deadline, official claim link.

Match

Your questionnaire answers decide which settlements fit you. New ones get a quick question.

File

Tap File claim to open the official settlement site and submit it yourself. ClaimScout never files for you: claims are sworn statements.

Get paid

The payout watcher checks your email and Spam and alerts you when money or a request arrives.

Install

One command on your PC. One scan on your phone.

The installer gets everything ClaimScout needs (even Python), starts it in the background, and ends with two QR codes: one to download the app, one to pair it.

Terminal (Linux)
$curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TheWiderLensInitiative/claimscout/main/install.sh | bash

Privacy

No servers. No accounts. Nothing to sell.

ClaimScout has no company servers. It's your PC talking to your phone. Read exactly what goes where →

Your data stays home

Your profile, matches and claims live in one folder on your PC. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Email, read-only, from the phone

Email passwords and tokens never leave your phone; they're sealed with an Android Keystore key. Only matching emails, trimmed, go to your PC.

Pinned, encrypted link

The phone talks to your PC over HTTPS pinned to your PC's own certificate, so nothing in between can listen in or pretend to be it.

Read every line

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is ClaimScout really free?

Yes. It's free and open source under the Apache 2.0 license: no subscription, no account, no paid features and no paid data APIs. The only thing that could ever cost money is an AI model you choose to pay for, and AI is optional (free local and free OpenRouter models work).

Does it file claims for me?

No, on purpose. A settlement claim is a sworn statement, usually "under penalty of perjury", so only you can say you qualify. ClaimScout opens the official claim form and has your proof and order numbers ready to copy. Filing takes a minute or two.

How does it get paid to me?

Settlements pay you directly, by the method you pick on the claim form or in a later "choose your payment" email: PayPal, Venmo, a virtual prepaid card, direct deposit or a check. ClaimScout never touches your money. Its job is to make sure you see those payment emails in time.

Is it safe to connect my email?

Email is read-only and searched from your phone, never from a server. Gmail uses an app password you can revoke anytime; Outlook uses Microsoft's own sign-in. Only emails that match a settlement are sent, trimmed, to your own PC. Your password never leaves the phone.

Which email providers work?

Gmail (with an app password) and Outlook, Hotmail and Microsoft 365. You can connect more than one account.

Does my PC have to stay on?

For the 6-hour settlement check and the 4-hour payout check, yes. ClaimScout is light (a small Python server), so an always-on desktop, laptop or mini PC is plenty. If the PC is off, the app catches up when it's back.

Can I use it away from home?

Yes. Install the free Tailscale app on your PC and phone. The pairing code includes the Tailscale address, and the app switches between home Wi-Fi and Tailscale automatically.

Is there an iPhone app?

Not yet. ClaimScout is Android only for now (Android 9+).

Where do the settlements come from?

The public listings on Top Class Actions, ClassAction.org and OpenClassActions, plus any community lists you add. Each listing links back to its source and the official settlement site. Always read the official notice before filing.
